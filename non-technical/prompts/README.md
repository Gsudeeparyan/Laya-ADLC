# Prompts

A prompt is just what you ask Copilot. You don't need special words. Ask the way you'd ask a capable new colleague who knows nothing about your work yet.

## The four parts of a good prompt

| Part | Say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | What you want | "Summarise this procedure..." |
| **Context** | Why, and who it's for | "...for a new starter in my team..." |
| **Source** | What to work from | "...using only the document I've uploaded..." |
| **Expectations** | Format, length, tone | "...in five plain bullets. If something isn't clear, list it as a question." |

Put together:

> Summarise this procedure for a new starter in my team, using only the document I've uploaded, in five plain bullets. If something isn't clear, list it as a question for me.

## Five habits that help most

1. **Give it the source.** Upload the document. Without one, it guesses.
2. **Say who it's for.** "For a member who has never claimed" changes everything.
3. **Ask it to flag what it isn't sure of.** "List anything you're unsure about" brings doubts into the open.
4. **Keep talking.** The first answer is a draft. Say "shorter", "friendlier" or "you missed the deadline".
5. **Tell it what you want, not just what you don't.** "Use short sentences" works better than "don't be wordy".

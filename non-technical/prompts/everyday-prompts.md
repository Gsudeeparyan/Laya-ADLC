# Everyday prompts

These work on the free version and on the paid licence. Copy one, change the [brackets], and upload your file where it says to. For a bigger job, the Laya agent named alongside will do it better.

## Writing

**Reply to an email**
> Here's an email I received: [paste, without names or personal details]. Write a reply that [says yes / asks for more time / explains the next step]. Keep it friendly, under 100 words, with the main point first.

**Make it shorter and clearer**
> Rewrite this in plain English for [who it's for]. Keep every fact and date exactly as it is. Use short sentences, and explain any term they might not know: [paste text]

**Change the tone**
> This email sounds [too blunt / too formal]. Make it [warmer / more professional] without changing what it asks for: [paste text]

**Say something difficult**
> I need to tell [a colleague / a supplier] that [the situation]. Give me three ways to say it, from gentlest to most direct, and tell me which you'd use and why.

**Start a document**
> I need a one-page [briefing note / proposal] for [who], who needs to decide [what]. Here's what I know: [bullets]. Draft it, and put [to confirm] wherever you need a fact I haven't given you.

*For more:* Laya Comms Writer, Laya Document Co-author.

## Summarising and reading

**Summarise a long document** (upload it)
> Summarise this for me in five bullets, then list any actions, deadlines and decisions. Use only this document. Tell me the page or section for each point.

**Find one thing** (upload it)
> What does this document say about [topic]? Quote the exact wording and give the section. If it doesn't cover it, say so.

**Compare two versions** (upload both)
> What changed between these two versions? List each change, and flag any where the meaning changed, such as "should" becoming "must", or a new date or amount.

**Explain it to me** (upload it)
> I'm new to this. Explain this document as you would to a colleague who has never seen it. What's it for, what does it need from me, and what should I watch out for?

*For more:* Laya Document Summariser.

## Spreadsheets

**Understand a spreadsheet** (upload it)
> What is this spreadsheet for? Walk me through each sheet and the main columns, and tell me anything that looks wrong.

**Explain a formula**
> Explain this Excel formula in plain words, and give me an example with made-up numbers: [paste formula]

**Find out why a total is wrong** (upload it)
> The total in [cell] should be about [amount] but it shows [amount]. Find out why. Tell me which rows or formulas cause the gap, and don't change anything yet.

**Get the story from the numbers** (upload it, with personal data removed first)
> What are the three most important things in this data for [who]? Show the numbers behind each one, and tell me anything that looks unreliable.

*For more:* Laya Excel Explainer, Laya Excel Expert, Laya Excel Insights. Take personal data out first with the Laya PII Guard.

## Meetings

**Prepare**
> I have a meeting about [topic] with [role, not name]. They'll probably care about [what]. What questions should I expect, and what should I bring?

**Agenda**
> Write a 30-minute agenda for a meeting to [decide what]. Give each item a time and an owner, and start with the decision we need.

**Actions from notes**
> Here are my meeting notes: [paste]. List what was decided, who's doing what by when, and what's still open. Mark anything without a clear owner as TBC.

*For more:* Laya Meeting Actions.

## Thinking something through

**Test a plan**
> Here's my plan: [describe]. What could go wrong? What am I assuming? Ask me the three questions you think I most need to answer.

**See the other side**
> I think we should [your view]. What's the strongest case against it, from someone who knows this area well?

**Weigh options**
> I'm choosing between [A] and [B]. What matters most is [what]. Lay out the pros and cons in a table, then recommend one and say what would change your mind.

*For more:* Laya Idea Refiner.

## Learning

**Explain a topic**
> Explain [topic] to me in plain English, as if I'm new to it. Then give me an example from an office job and a three-question quiz.

**Prepare for something new**
> I'm starting to work on [area]. What are the ten things someone new should know? Mark which you're sure of, so I can check the rest.

## Checking

**Check an AI answer**
> Here's an answer Copilot gave me: [paste]. List every fact and number in it I should check before relying on it, and where I could check each one.

**Check a draft against its source** (upload both)
> Check this draft against the source. List anything the draft says that the source doesn't, anything it left out that matters, and any number that doesn't match.

*For more:* Laya Second Check.

# Run a 30-minute team session

For team leads and AI champions. No slides or technical knowledge are needed. You only need a screen, Copilot and something real but low-risk to try.

**The aim:** by the end, everyone has used Copilot on something from their own work, checked the result, and knows what's off limits.

**Before the session:**
- Ask people to read the [AI basics](ai-basics.md), and print or share the [one-page rules](one-page-rules.md).
- Open your department's [playbook](../by-role/README.md) and pick two jobs to try.
- Prepare a real, low-risk example with no personal data. A public document or an old internal guide works well.
- Find out who has the free version and who has the paid licence.

## The session

**5 minutes: why we're doing this**
- AI is here to take the slog out of some work, not to replace judgement. Say what it means for your team.
- The two things that matter: you're responsible for what you send, and telling someone quickly when it goes wrong is what we want.

**10 minutes: try it together**
1. Show the green shield.
2. Ask a weak prompt on screen ("summarise this"), and look at the result.
3. Ask the same with the four parts: goal, context, source, expectations. Compare.
4. Find something it got wrong or left out. There's nearly always something. **Finding it is the most useful moment of the session.**
5. Tell it what to fix.

**10 minutes: everyone tries**
Pairs are fine. Each person picks a job from the playbook and tries it. Walk round. Ask "How would you check that?" rather than "Is it good?"

**5 minutes: agree as a team**
Write down three things:
- One job we'll start using AI for this month.
- One thing we'll always check.
- One thing we won't use it for.

Share this with your AI champion, and pick a date in a month to look at how it's going.

## Questions people ask

**"Will this replace my job?"**
Don't guess. Say what you know about Laya's plans, and pass the question on to your manager if you don't **[to confirm: agreed company message]**. What's true today: Copilot helps with drafting and reading, and it needs a person to check and decide.

**"Do I have to use it?"**
Be honest about what's expected in your team. Using it badly is worse than not using it.

**"Should I tell people when I've used it?"**
Be open with your team. For anything customer-facing or regulated, follow your team's approval route. What matters most is that you've checked it.

**"Is it reading all my emails?"**
On the free version, only in Outlook's Copilot Chat when you ask, and only your own. On the paid licence, it can search what you already have access to, when you ask. It isn't used to monitor you **[to confirm with HR: Laya's position on monitoring]**, but chats are logged, like email.

**"What if it gets something wrong and I've already sent it?"**
Tell your manager. See [When something goes wrong](../staff-guide/when-something-goes-wrong.md).

**"Why does my colleague have more features?"**
Some people have the paid licence. Point them at the free routes in [Which Copilot do I have?](../staff-guide/which-copilot-do-i-have.md)

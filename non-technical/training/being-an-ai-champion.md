# Being an AI champion

Each team has someone who's a bit ahead with AI, and helps the others. You don't need to be technical. You need to be curious, patient and honest about what doesn't work.

## What you do

**Help people get started.** Show them where things are, answer questions, and sit with someone for ten minutes on a real task.

**Run a team session.** Use the [session plan](run-a-team-session.md) at the start, and repeat it for new starters.

**Share what works.** Collect the prompts that work, and pass them on. Ten minutes at a team meeting beats a long document.

**Pass on what doesn't.** If an agent keeps getting something wrong, tell the library owner, with the exact prompt and what went wrong. **Take any personal data out first.**

**Keep the playbook honest.** Tell the library owner what's missing from your department's playbook, or what doesn't match how your team really works.

**Spot problems.** If you see risky use, such as personal data, AI deciding about people, or a tool that isn't approved, raise it kindly and early. Point them to the Laya Privacy & AI Check, or your manager.

## What you don't do

- **You don't approve things.** You don't decide what's allowed. The DPO, Compliance and the AI owner do.
- **You don't check everyone's work.** Each person stays responsible for their own.
- **You don't monitor people.** You help when asked, and you raise concerns about the work, not by watching what colleagues do.

## Support for the free and paid groups

Your team may have both. For each thing you show, also show the free route. A paid licence holder can build agents for the team: see [how to deploy the agents](../admin/deploy-agents.md), and check with IT first.

## How much time

About an hour a week at first, less later on. Agree the time with your manager, so it's real work and not a favour.

## Every month, tell the library owner

1. Roughly how many people in your team use Copilot regularly.
2. The best thing someone did with it, with the prompt.
3. The worst problem, and what fixed it.
4. What's missing.

No names are needed, and please don't track individuals.

# AI basics

A 20-minute read for everyone at Laya. No technical background needed.

## 1. What Copilot actually is

Copilot is built on a large language model. It has learned patterns from a huge amount of text, and it predicts what words should come next. That makes it very good with language: drafting, rewriting, summarising, explaining.

It doesn't "know" things the way a person does, and it doesn't check facts unless it's given a source. When it doesn't know, it often doesn't say so. It writes something that sounds right.

**A useful picture:** think of a very fast, very willing new colleague. They've read a lot, they're great at first drafts, they don't know Laya, they never say "I'm not sure", and they'll sometimes make things up to be helpful. You wouldn't send their work out without reading it. Treat Copilot the same way.

## 2. What it's good at, and what it isn't

| Good at | Weak at |
|---|---|
| First drafts | Being right about facts it hasn't been given |
| Rewriting in plainer words or a different tone | Laya's own rules, unless you give it the document |
| Summarising what you give it | Knowing what's changed recently |
| Explaining a formula or a document | Judgement: what's fair, what's wise, what matters here |
| Spotting patterns in data you upload | Knowing when it's wrong |

## 3. How it goes wrong

**It makes things up.** Article numbers, statistics, quotes, policy names and links can all look real and not exist. People call this "hallucination".

**It sounds sure either way.** A wrong answer reads just like a right one. Confidence tells you nothing.

**It can drift when summarising.** "Should" can become "must". A deadline can change, and an exception can vanish.

**It can reflect bias.** It learned from text written by people, so it can repeat unfair assumptions about groups of people.

**It can agree too easily.** If you suggest an answer, it tends to go along with it, so ask it to argue the other side.

**Files can trick it.** A document or email can contain hidden instructions aimed at the AI. If Copilot does something odd after reading a file, stop and tell the IT service desk.

**People get used to trusting it.** The more often it's right, the less carefully we check. This is the risk that grows with time.

## 4. How to check its work

The more it matters, the harder you check.

1. **Go to the source.** Open the document it used and find the wording yourself.
2. **Check every number, date, name and reference.** These are the most common errors, and the most costly.
3. **Recalculate anything that matters.** Do the sum yourself on two or three figures.
4. **Read it as the person who'll get it.** Would it make sense to them? Could it mislead?
5. **Ask for a second opinion.** Use the **Laya Second Check**, or better, a colleague.

If you couldn't explain it or defend it yourself, don't send it.

## 5. Keeping data safe

**Look for the green shield** at the top of Copilot. It means your chat has Microsoft's enterprise data protection, so what you type isn't used to train AI models. Without the shield, don't use work information. Use only Laya's Copilot for work: not personal accounts, not other AI websites.

**Keep personal data out unless the job needs it.** You can usually get what you want without it: "a member with a query about a waiting period" works as well as a name. If the job needs a file with personal data, clean it first with the **Laya PII Guard**.

**Some data needs extra care.** Health information is the most sensitive data Laya holds, and the law protects it more strongly. So does information about children. Be careful with PPS numbers, bank details and HR files too.

**Your chats aren't private.** They're logged and can be reviewed by Laya, like email.

## 6. What stays with people

Some decisions should never be left to AI, whatever it suggests:

- whether a claim is paid, or what cover someone has;
- a price, or a risk assessment of a person;
- the outcome of a complaint;
- whether someone gets a job, a promotion or a warning;
- whether someone is committing fraud.

EU law lists some of these uses as high-risk, including AI used for pricing and risk assessment in health insurance, and for hiring. If someone proposes AI for any of these, use the **Laya Privacy & AI Check** and speak to your manager.

**You're accountable.** "Copilot wrote it" isn't an explanation. If your name is on it, it's yours.

## 7. Getting good results

Every good prompt has four parts: your **goal**, the **context**, the **source** and your **expectations**.

> Summarise this procedure *(goal)* for a new starter in my team *(context)*, using only the document I've uploaded *(source)*, in five plain bullets. List anything unclear as a question *(expectations)*.

And keep talking. The first answer is a draft. Say "shorter", "friendlier", or "you missed the deadline". The **Laya Prompt Coach** can help.

## 8. Free or paid?

Most people have the free version. You bring the information to it, by uploading a file or pasting text. Those with the paid licence get Copilot inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams, and it can find their work information for them.

Neither is more careful than the other, and both need checking. See [Which Copilot do I have?](../staff-guide/which-copilot-do-i-have.md)

## 9. When something goes wrong

Stop, keep what you have, and tell someone the same day: your manager, and the Data Protection Officer if personal data is involved. It's about speed, not blame. See [When something goes wrong](../staff-guide/when-something-goes-wrong.md).

## Try it now (5 minutes)

1. Open Copilot and find the green shield.
2. Pick something real but low-risk, such as an email you need to write, with no personal details.
3. Ask for it using the four parts.
4. Tell it one thing to change.
5. Read the result as if you were the person receiving it.

Then take the [quick quiz](quiz.md).

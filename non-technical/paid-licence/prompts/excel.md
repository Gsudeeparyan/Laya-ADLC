# Copilot in Excel

Copilot works best when your data is formatted as a table, with one header row and no merged cells. Select your data and press **Ctrl+T**. Take personal data out first if the job doesn't need it.

## Understand it

> What does this data show? Give me the three most important things, with the numbers.

> Explain what the formula in this column does, in plain words.

> Which values look unusual or wrong, and why?

## Analyse it

> Show total [amount] by [category] and by month as a PivotTable on a new sheet.

> How has [measure] changed compared with the same period last year? Is it like for like?

> Which [teams / categories] make up 80% of the total?

## Add to it

> Add a column that shows how many days it's been since [date column], with a formula.

> Highlight rows where [status] is overdue and [amount] is over [value].

> Suggest a chart that shows [what], and add it.

## Before you use it

- **Check any number going to someone else.** Pick two or three, and work them out yourself or ask the **Laya Second Check**.
- **Check for like-for-like comparisons and small groups.** A jump from 2 to 4 isn't a trend. Reports about people leave out groups smaller than 5.
- **Is it a Laya AI-safe workbook** (it has a Change Log sheet)? For changes to its structure, use the **Laya Excel Expert**, so its memory stays correct. Copilot in Excel doesn't update it.
- **Save a copy first** for big changes. SharePoint keeps old versions if you need to go back.
